    What this does not show
    • Beds change minute by minute in the simulation; the real data is weekly. The simulation starts every ICU at its real reported number and keeps the weekly average equal to it.
    • Road times are free-flow (no traffic) from OpenStreetMap. Ambulance availability is not modelled.

    • When the nearest hospital has room, phoning it is as fast or faster. MAHROS helps most when nearby hospitals are full — the Evidence tab measures how often.

      What is this?
      A working prototype of the coordination service a state health department could run. In an emergency it finds a patient a bed by asking every capable hospital at once. Every week it checks which hospital reports can be believed, warns where hospitals are about to fill up, and plans where patients, nurses and equipment should go. Every request, answer and decision is written into a record book that cannot be quietly changed.

      Who the agents are

      AgentHow manyWhat it knowsWhat it decides
      Hospital agentone per hospitalOnly its own private state: beds in use, nurses on shift, ventilators, cath-lab rooms, its own policy of beds kept backWhether to bid for an emergency patient (and why not, if it declines); whether to accept a weekly transfer
      Data StewardoneEvery hospital's weekly reports and its historyWhich reports to set aside, with the rule that fired; which emergency bids must be confirmed by phone
      ForecasteroneTrusted reports up to this weekNext week's occupancy for each hospital, with a 90% range
      Emergency coordinatorone per statePublic facts only: locations, road times, CMS capability, weekly reportsWho to ask, which bid wins (time to definitive care first)
      Regional coordinatorone per stateForecasts, verdicts, staff estimates, equipment inventoryThe weekly patient plan — suggestions only. The nurse and equipment plan is computed alongside it with the same kind of optimiser.

      No agent can read another's private state. They talk only through messages, and the messages of every emergency case and every weekly cycle go into the record book. The surge early warning is a model run on the same reports, not a separate agent.

      What MAHROS does not do
      • It is not deployed and moves no real patients. Emergency timings come from a simulation grounded in real data; weekly plans are scored against what hospitals really reported next.
      • It does not see individual patients — only hospital totals. No patient data is used anywhere.
      • It does not override hospitals. Every transfer needs the receiving hospital's own yes.
      • It does not predict pandemics. The early warning is about hospital capacity, from hospital reports.

        Can anyone quietly change the record afterwards?
        No. MAHROS writes every request, answer and decision into a record book in pen. Each line carries a fingerprint of the line before it. After a bad outcome nobody can claim "we were never asked": the book shows the request at 10:42 and the refusal at 10:44, and proves neither line was changed since.

        What this does not show
        • The book proves nothing was changed after it was written. It cannot prove a line was true when written — that is the Data Steward's job.
        • It is held by the coordinating authority and can be checked by anyone with the database. It is not a public blockchain, and does not need to be.
        • Emergency lines carry the simulated clock time (labelled "simulated"); the real time of writing is stored inside each line too.
        What should a careful reader keep in mind?
        The weekly data is real and official, but self-reported and ends in April 2024. Emergency timings are a simulation grounded in that data. Staff numbers are estimates and equipment is entered. Nothing here moved a real patient.
