    What you are watching
    • Every dot is a real hospital, and every number is the real report it sent the U.S. government that week. The agents are replaying those weeks.
    • Moves are suggestions, checked against what the hospitals really reported the following week. No real patient was moved.
    • "Patients" means the average number of patients during the week.
    What is this?
    A platform a state health department could run: every week it checks which hospital reports can be believed, predicts which hospitals will be too full, and proposes moving patients to nearby hospitals with room. Each hospital decides for itself whether to accept.

    How one week works — five agents, one record

    STEP 1
    Hospital agents
    Each hospital submits its numbers: beds, patients, ICU.

    STEP 2

    Marks every report GREEN AMBER RED and says why.

    STEP 3
    Forecaster
    Predicts next week's occupancy, with a 90% range.
    →
    STEP 4
    State coordinator
    Proposes transfers from too-full to nearby hospitals with room.
    →
    STEP 5
    Hospitals decide
    Accept only what their real numbers allow. Everything is sealed in the audit log.

    What MAHROS does not do
    • It does not move real patients. Every transfer here is a proposal tested against what hospitals reported the following week.
    • It does not see individual patients — only weekly hospital totals. No patient data is used anywhere.
    • It does not override hospitals. A proposal becomes a transfer only if both hospitals' real numbers allow it.

      Does the answer depend on our assumptions?

      What this does not show
      • No patient was actually moved. Numbers are what could have been absorbed, checked against what hospitals reported the following week.
      • It ignores clinical suitability (specialty, how sick the patient is), ambulance availability and patient choice. Real absorption would be lower.
      • Distance is straight-line. The Houston case study checks this against real driving times.
      • "Safe level" (90% full), the 80 km radius and the 5%-per-week cap are our assumptions; the sensitivity table shows how much they matter.
      Can anyone quietly change the record afterwards?
      No. Every report, verdict, forecast, proposal and answer is chained together by fingerprints (hashes). Change one number anywhere and every check after it fails.

      What this does not show
      • The log proves nothing was changed after it was written. It cannot prove a report was true when written — that is the Data Steward's job.
      • It is a ledger held by the coordinating authority and checkable by anyone with the entries. It is not a public blockchain, and does not need to be.
      What should a careful reader keep in mind?
      The data is real and official, but it is weekly, self-reported and ends in April 2024. The transfers are proposals, scored against what really happened next — not transfers that took place.
